The category I would now use
Based on the design evidence, my preferred working category is:

Customer-owned, integrated cloud-native application platform.

I would avoid calling Kamarkat an Infrastructure OS as the primary category. That term risks suggesting a general-purpose infrastructure operating system, extensive provider support and complete automation of all lifecycle operations.

For customer-facing messaging, the more useful language is:
PROPOSED KAMARKAT POSITIONING
Your startup's complete cloud-native foundation. Pre-integrated. Versioned. Yours to operate.
Kamarkat provisions and connects the infrastructure your applications depend on—from Kubernetes and GitOps to identity, secrets, databases, networking and observability—in your own cloud account.

Your team supplies its infrastructure and application configuration. Kamarkat supplies the platform foundation and the standard integrations, so you can focus on developing your product instead of assembling the infrastructure stack.

This is a more specific statement than merely promising an open-source platform or a golden path.
Final conclusions
I would rank Kamarkat's design-based differentiation this way:
1.
Application-to-infrastructure integration

This is the strongest startup-facing proposition: application declarations connect to standard platform services rather than leaving developers to integrate every service themselves.


2.
One operating model from infrastructure to application delivery

The same customer-owned GitOps foundation governs cluster lifecycle, environments, platform configuration and applications.


3.
Independence from a vendor-hosted management plane

Customers operate the platform in infrastructure they control, while using Kamarkat's versioned distribution.

Kamarkat gives startups a pre-integrated cloud-native platform, from infrastructure provisioning to application delivery, in a box they own—without requiring them to assemble and maintain the entire platform stack themselves.

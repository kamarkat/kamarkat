I support this direction. “Agentic Cloud-Native Platform OS” is a stronger long-term positioning for Kamarkat than simply “cloud infrastructure platform.”

The addition of an integrated agent, MCP registry and skills registry changes the product vision in an important way.

Kamarkat would no longer be positioned solely as a platform that provisions and operates cloud-native infrastructure. It would also provide a standardized foundation through which AI agents can understand, interact with and manage that infrastructure.

That creates a compelling combination:

A pre-integrated cloud-native platform, owned by the business, designed to be operated by both engineers and AI agents.

However, there is one distinction I would preserve: the agentic capabilities are part of the intended product direction, while TM's architecture report established the existing Kubernetes, GitOps and platform-service integrations. We should not present the future agentic layer as already implemented.

What could differentiate it is that the agents operate a platform whose components, relationships and desired state are already standardized by Kamarkat.

## The three-layer product vision

Agentic operations layer — planned

Integrated agents, MCP registry and skills registry for discovering and performing platform operations.

Integrated platform OS — implemented design

GitOps, cluster lifecycle, identity, secrets, databases, gateway, networking and observability working together.

Business-owned infrastructure — implemented design

The management cluster and supported workloads operate in infrastructure controlled by the business.

The important architectural principle is that agents should operate through Kamarkat's established declarative contracts, rather than becoming a second, competing source of infrastructure state.

For example, an agent could eventually investigate an unhealthy workload, identify the relevant cluster and application declarations, propose a correction and use the existing GitOps workflow to apply an authorized change.

That would be a meaningful agentic operating experience—not merely exposing `kubectl` through an MCP server.

I support using “for businesses” in the long-term product identity. It avoids positioning Kamarkat as something a company must outgrow after its startup phase. But I would retain startups and small engineering teams as the initial target customer until broader demand is established.

## The one distinction we must preserve

An agent registry, MCP registry and skills registry are foundations for agentic operations. They do not, on their own, establish that agents can safely and reliably operate infrastructure.

The value comes when agents can use those capabilities to act through the platform's existing authorization, reconciliation and operational boundaries.

My recommendation is to adopt “Agentic Cloud-Native Platform OS” as Kamarkat's product vision now, while keeping public present-tense capability claims tied to what has actually been implemented.

The resulting proposition is substantially more specific:

Kamarkat aims to give businesses one pre-integrated, customer-owned cloud-native operating foundation—designed for engineers and AI agents to manage together, without every business having to assemble its own platform stack.
